What it is

The Director’s Notebook keeps a scene’s breakdown, strategy, floor plan and shot list in one place. You work on one scene at a time, and the four tabs at the top are four views of that same scene.

It runs in a browser and can be installed as an app. Once it has been opened on a device it keeps working without a connection.

Installing it

Where: app.thedirectorsnotebook.com

On the iPad

  1. Open the address in Safari.
  2. Tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
  3. Open it from the Home Screen from then on.

On the Mac

  1. Open the address in Chrome.
  2. Use Chrome’s menu to install the page as an app, then keep it in the Dock.

The app updates itself. When a new version is ready it reloads at a quiet moment, or shows a Reload bar if you are in the middle of something. The version date is in Menu › Sync & Backup.

The screen

  1. 1Menu. Scenes, importing, exporting, sync and backups.
  2. 2Folder and scene. Shows where you are. Tap it to move to another scene.
  3. 3The four tabs. Breakdown, Strategy, Floor Plan, Shot List.
  4. 4Prep · Scout · Set. The same scene, arranged for the stage of work you are in.
  5. 5Status. Says whether your work is saved or synced. Tap it to sign in or see Sync.
  6. 6Add. Characters, extras, cameras, props, set, lighting, annotations, a background image, and Measure a Distance.
  7. 7Pages and Layers. Pages split a scene’s blocking. Layers shows, hides, fades and locks each kind of piece.
  8. 8Undo and Redo, then Select, Draw, Frames, Fit to Screen and Rotate the Floor Plan.
  9. 9Playback. Turn on the animation view, play the blocking, and step through its Times.
  10. 10Shot List panel. Shows or hides the list beside the plan.

The card at the top left of the plan is the scene’s heading. Tap it to add scene info.

Scenes and folders

Scenes live in folders. A folder is usually one episode or one film, and importing a script makes a folder for it.

Where: tap the folder and scene name in the top bar, or Menu › Open Scene…. On the Breakdown, Strategy and Shot List tabs the Library sits down the left side.
  • New scene and New folder are at the top of the Library.
  • Drag a scene by its grip to move it into a folder. The ••• beside a scene has the rest.
  • Scenes outside any folder are listed under Not in a folder.
  • The Library also holds Templates and the Trash.

The four tabs

Each tab has its own chapter. Prep, Scout and Set are covered in chapters 9 and 10.

Saving, Undo and earlier versions

There is no Save button. Work is saved as you go, and the status at the top right says so.

  • Undo and Redo are on the toolbar.
  • Earlier Versions… keeps a copy every five minutes while you work, covering about the last six hours, plus one a day for 30 days. Restoring one replaces what is there now, and you can undo that.
  • Save a Version Now… keeps a copy under a name you choose until you delete it.
  • Back Up Everything makes a file you can keep anywhere. Restore from Backup… reads one back in.
Where: Menu › Sync & Backup.

What it will not do: earlier versions live on the device you are using. They do not travel to your other devices. For a copy that does, use Back Up Everything.

Signing in and sync

Signing in keeps the same scenes on every device. Without it, everything still works and stays on the one device.

Where: tap the status at the top right.
  1. The very first time, enter an email and password and tap Create account.
  2. On your other device, Sign in with the same email and password.
  • This is the app’s own account. It is separate from any other account you have.
  • Work done offline is sent when you are back online.
  • The Sync window shows when the cloud last confirmed your work, with Sync now and Sign out. Signing out leaves your work on the device.
  • If sync is limited to approved people, the owner adds each exact email under Approved people….

Import a Script

Makes a folder with one scene for every scene in the script, and a character for everyone who speaks.

Where: Menu › Import › Import a Script…. Inside a folder’s window it is Import script….
  1. Choose the script’s PDF.
  2. Name the new folder, and choose whether it sits at the top level or inside another folder.
  3. Check the characters. Untick anything that is not a character, and set each one’s sex. guess marks the ones the app worked out from the script, so look at those.
  4. Leave the two boxes checked to have saved set templates added at matching locations, and each scene’s characters placed in the scene ready to block.
  5. Tap Import.

Each scene arrives with its number, heading and script pages. One Undo takes the whole import back.

What it will not do: it reads the text inside the PDF. A scanned script, which is only pictures of pages, has no text to read.

Scene info

The facts about a scene that appear on its pages and printouts.

Where: tap the scene’s card at the top left of the plan, or Menu › This Scene › Scene Info…, or ⌘I. The Breakdown and Strategy tabs have a Scene info button too.
  • Scene number, title and scene heading.
  • Episode or code, shoot day and shoot date.
  • Script pages, in eighths.
  • Act or sequence that starts at this scene. Type a name here, such as ACT TWO, and the Spine shows the act beginning at this scene.
  • Synopsis.

Breakdown

The Breakdown tab asks one question: what does the scene mean?

  • The columns are Synopsis, Snapshot, Imagery & Tone, The Core, Elevating the Scene and Pitfalls. Each starts with prompts, such as Scene Needs and Actor Direction Tips under The Core.
  • + Label adds a prompt of your own to a column.
  • Columns… changes the columns themselves.
  • Story marks the scene as A, B, C or D story. Edit… changes that list.
  • Questions column adds a column for open questions. They are gathered for the whole folder in the Overview.
  • B, • List and 1. List format what you type.

Strategy

The Strategy tab asks the next question: how do we make the meaning visible?

  • The sections are Power Dynamics (start, end and main event), Camera Strategy, Key Visuals, Blocking Rationale, Arc of Scene and Geometry, and Story and Tone Meeting Notes.
  • + Add a section adds your own. The ⋯ on a section has its options.
  • + Add a cut to another scene records a cut you are planning between this scene and another.

Import a Breakdown and Strategy

Brings in breakdowns and strategies you have already written in Word.

Where: Menu › Import › Import a Breakdown and Strategy…. On the Breakdown tab it is Import from Word….
  • It reads a Word file (.docx) and looks for Director’s Notebook and Shooting Strategy pages.
  • Pages are matched to scenes in the folder by scene number. A scene that does not exist yet is made.
  • A scene that already has writing in it is left alone unless you choose to replace it.

What it will not do: it cannot open the older .doc format. Save the file as .docx first.

Checklist

A to-do list that belongs to one scene. Add items, tick them off, and clear the finished ones with Remove Finished.

Where: Menu › This Scene › Checklist…, or the Checklist button on the Breakdown and Strategy tabs.

The folder’s Overview

Shows the whole episode or film at once, in four views.

Where: Menu › the line that names your folder, ending in Overview & Questions….
ViewWhat it shows
ScenesEach scene with its progress on the Breakdown, Strategy, Floor Plan and Shot List, and how many questions are open.
SpineThe scenes as blocks in story order, sized by page count, setups or time estimate, with a band for day or night. Tap a scene to see what it is, or to start an act or a sequence there. Below it, where each story lives.
CutsThe last frame of each scene beside the first frame of the next, so you can judge every cut between scenes.
QuestionsEverything written in the scenes’ Questions columns.

Export PDF… in the Overview prints the view you are on.

Add Set

Everything that makes up the place: walls, doors, windows, stairs, streets, water and whole buildings.

Where: Add › Add Set…, or press S.
  • Tap a piece and it lands in the middle of the view. Drag it where you want it.
  • Search set pieces finds one by name. Edit Favorites keeps the ones you use most at the top.
  • The three blue buttons are the larger tools, each covered below.
GroupPieces
Walls and doorsWall Tool, Window, Open Door, Closed Door, Double Open Door, Double Closed Door, Medium Opening, Prison Bars, Stairs, Round Column, Square Pillar, Railing, Fence, Archway, Curtain, Elevator
Streets, ground and waterStreet (2 lanes), Avenue (4 lanes), Intersection (4-way), Intersection (T), Sidewalk, Crosswalk, Parking Lot, Platform Edge, Fountain, Swimming Pool, Pond, Dock
BuildingsHouses from a Craftsman Bungalow to a Row of Brownstones, plus a Diner, Corner Store, Gas Station, Church and more

Walls

Where: Add › Add Set… › Wall Tool.
  1. Tap to place each corner.
  2. The wall finishes by itself when it meets another corner. Or tap Done.

Tap a wall for its menu:

  • Move All Joined Walls moves the room as one.
  • Unhook This Wall frees it from its neighbors.
  • Add a Corner Here and Remove Nearest Corner reshape it.
  • Add Side Walls adds a wall at right angles from each end, making three sides of a room.

Doors and windows

Drag a door or window onto a wall and it attaches, cutting its own opening. It then travels with the wall.

  • Flip to Other Side swings a door the other way.
  • Mirror moves the hinge to the other jamb.
  • Change Type… swaps it, for example an open door for a closed one.
  • Detach from Wall lets it stand free.
  • Allow Resizing, Keep Proportions and Reset Size control its size.

Draw a Building

For a building seen from above, drawn to the size you want.

Where: Add › Add Set… › Draw a Building…
  1. Choose Rectangle and drag from one corner to the opposite one, or choose Trace Corners and tap each corner, then Done.
  2. Lengths show in feet as you draw.
  3. In its menu, type an exact Width and Depth, and choose Flat Roof or Gable Roof.
  4. Mark the Front…, then tap the side that faces the street. Door on the Front puts a door there.
  • Selected, it has a handle on each corner and a plus on each side to add a corner.
  • Doors and windows attach to its walls, and it casts a shadow when the sun is on.

What it will not do: roofs are flat or gable only, and Roof On belongs to the building, so it is the same in every scene that shares it.

Templates

Ready-made sets and stagings to start from, and a way to keep your own.

Where: Menu › This Scene › Add a Template to This Scene…, and Save as Template….
KindTemplates
Indoor setsBar, Bedroom, Conference Room, Diner, Hallway, Hospital Room, Interrogation Room, Kitchen, Living Room, Office, Precinct Bullpen
Outdoor setsAlley, Park, Parking Lot, Street Corner
Two charactersAcross a Table, Deep Staging, Face to Face, Front Seat of a Car, Side by Side at a Bar, Walk and Talk

Four or moreCircle Standing, Dinner Table

Save as Template… keeps the open scene’s set under a name. Give it the location it is for, and scenes whose heading matches are offered it, including scenes from scripts you import later. You can keep the characters and cameras with it.

Overhead from an Address

Draws the block around a real address: its streets, lots, houses, woods and water, to scale, with north marked.

Where: Add › Add Set… › Overhead from an Address…
  1. Type the address and tap Draw.
  2. To see the real ground, open Layers and turn on Aerial Photo under Background Image.
  • It needs a connection, because it reads public map data.
  • Trees and bushes are added for dressing. Uncheck that in the window if you want it bare.
  • The streets come from OpenStreetMap. If you publish a plan made this way, credit OpenStreetMap.

What it will not do: it knows outlines, not what is on a lot. A pool or a driveway will not appear, so use the Aerial Photo to place those. It has been tested with New Jersey addresses; elsewhere it may find less.

Set from a Director’s Plan

Puts the art department’s plan under the floor at true size, and builds what it can read from it as pieces.

Where: Add › Add Set… › Set from a Director’s Plan…
  1. Choose the PDF. If it has several pages, tap the one you want.
  2. Check the scale, then tap Place the Plan.
  3. If shapes repeat on the plan, say what each one is: a stock piece, a plain shape you can name, or leave it out.
  • It reads solid walls, glass walls, doors from their arcs, windows and stairs, and places furniture by size.
  • The plan’s opacity is the Background Image slider in Layers.
  • One Undo takes back the plan and everything that came with it.

What it will not do: it reads square-on walls only, not angled ones. It does not read which way a piece faces, and it misses some doors and chairs. Treat the result as a first pass to correct against the plan underneath.

Background image

Any picture can sit under the plan to trace over: a location photo, a sketch, a plan.

Where: Add › Background Image….

After you add one, the app offers to use it in the other scenes at the same place. Its opacity is in Layers.

Locations

One set shared by every scene that plays there. Change it once and it changes everywhere.

Where: Menu › This Scene › Save This Set as a Location…. Other scenes join with Use a Location….
  • Shared: walls, set pieces, props and the plan image.
  • Kept by each scene: characters, cameras, lights, notes and drawings.
  • Scenes at the same place are offered when you save. A linked scene gets this set in place of its own.

Layers

Shows, fades and locks each kind of piece.

Where: Layers, on the toolbar.
  • The layers are Characters, Arrows, Cameras, Storyboards, Props, Set, Lighting, Drawings and Labels. Each has an opacity slider and a lock. Lock all locks everything.
  • Visibility switches: Locked Items, Shot Labels, Shot Descriptions, Smart Guides, Sun & Shadows, Night Look and Background Image.

For example, fade the Set layer while you block, or lock it so a wall cannot be nudged by accident.

Measure a Distance

Where: Add › Measure a Distance.

Tap two points and the distance between them shows in feet. Tap Done to finish.

Working with a piece

Where: the piece’s menu. On the iPad, tap a selected piece again. On a computer, select it and press Shift, or right-click.
  • Moves To… and Comes From… give a piece positions of its own, so a car can drive or a chair can be pulled out.
  • Add Label… names it on the plan.
  • Change Type… swaps it for another piece in the same spot.
  • Mirror flips it. Allow Resizing and Keep Proportions control stretching, and Reset Size puts it back.
  • Color recolors it.
  • Lock in Place stops it moving by accident.
  • Bring to Front and Send to Back settle what sits on top.

Details

Where: the piece’s menu › Details….

Details opens a panel at the side for the finer settings of whatever is selected.

  • A prop has its Label, its Fill color and the pages it shows on.
  • A light adds the Beam slider.
  • A character or a camera has a fuller panel. See Chapters 5 and 6.

My Props and the Plain Shape

  • The Plain Shape is a simple block you can size and label, for anything the library does not have.
  • My Props are pieces you have made yourself. Select several pieces, open the menu and choose Save as My Prop…. Name it, and it appears under My Props in Add Prop on every device signed in to your account.
  • Characters, cameras, drawings and uploaded images cannot be saved in a prop. They stay with their scene.
  • Plain shapes you name while reading a director’s plan are kept in My Props too.

Notes and drawings

Where: Add › Add Annotation…, or press T. Freehand drawing is Draw on the toolbar.
  • Annotations are Text, Mask, Straight Arrow and Curved Arrow.
  • Draw has Pen, Arrow, Circle and Erase. Tap Done when you finish.
  • Menu › This Scene › Tidy Labels on This Page spreads labels out so they do not overlap.

Add a character

Where: Add › Add Character…, or press C.
  • Pick someone from the cast in this project, or type a new name.
  • A character keeps their name and color in every scene of the folder.

Add Extras… places a group of background people at once. You choose how many, a group name, their arrangement and color. They are numbered, drawn a little smaller than the cast, and can walk like anyone else.

Positions and moves

Where: the character’s menu. Tapping a position’s number opens it too. W is Moves To and F is Comes From.
  1. Tap Moves To…
  2. Tap where the character goes next.

Each stop is a numbered position, joined by a dashed path. Comes From… adds a position before the one you tapped.

  • Add a Turn (R) turns the character on the spot. Drag its number to point it.
  • Posture is Standing, Seated, Kneeling or Lying.
  • Put On… or Put In… appears when a character is over a piece or a vehicle. It attaches them, so moving or turning the piece carries them. Take Off… detaches them.
  • 180° Line To… (X) draws the line between two characters.
  • Delete Position removes one stop. Delete All removes the character from the scene.
  • Character Details… opens a panel for the name, color and type, the position you tapped, and the pages the character shows on. The type marks the figure: one stripe for male, three for female.

Draw the Walk

For a path that is not a straight line: around furniture, through a doorway, along a curve.

Where: the character’s menu › Moves To… › Draw the Walk in the bar at the top.

Times and playback

A Time is a beat of the scene. Everything set to play in the same Time moves together.

Where: the sun button on the toolbar, with Play and the Times beside it.
  • Tap the sun button to turn on the animation view.
  • Tap a Time’s number, then tap a position to put it in that Time. Tap it again to take it out.
  • Plays in Time in a character’s or camera’s menu does the same job from the plan.
  • Play, or Space, plays the blocking.
  • The eye button shows the other Times faded, or hides them.
  • Tap the sun again, or press Esc, to go back to editing.

A Time’s menu: speed, labels and order

Where: in the animation view, tap the Time you are on.
  • Insert Time Before and Insert Time After make room for a new beat. Delete Time removes one.
  • Insert Section Label… names a stretch of the scene on the toolbar.
  • Actor Speed and Camera Speed set how fast things move in this Time. Turn off Same Speed to set them apart.
  • Slow, Normal and Fast set how fast playback runs. Loop plays it again and again.
  • Save as Video… records the blocking. See Chapter 11.

Pages and blocking plans

Where: the Pages button on the toolbar.
  • Pages split a long scene’s blocking into parts. All Pages shows everything at once. New Page… adds one, and each piece’s menu says which pages it is on.
  • Blocking plans are alternatives for the same scene. New Plan from This One copies Plan A so you can try another way without losing the first.

Selecting several things

  • Select on the toolbar, or G, draws a lasso around pieces.
  • Shift-click adds a piece to the selection or takes it out.
  • ⌘G groups the selection so it moves as one.

Clear Moves

Starts the blocking over without rebuilding the set or placing anyone again.

Where: Menu › This Scene › Clear Moves….
  • Choose camera moves, character moves, or both.
  • For characters, keep where they end or where they start.
  • With something selected, you can clear only that.
  • Shots, descriptions and storyboards stay. Locked pieces are left alone. One Undo brings everything back.

Cameras and Shots

Every camera on the plan is a shot on the list. This chapter covers placing cameras, moving them and describing what they see.

In this chapter

  2. The camera’s menu
  3. Describe the shot
  4. Lenses on the plan
  5. Track With
  6. Storyboard frames

Add a camera

Where: Add › Add Camera…, or press A. The Shot List panel has + Add camera setup.
  • New setup asks how many cameras and gives the setup the next number and color.
  • Another camera on setup… adds a B or C camera to a setup you have.

A shot is named by its setup and camera, such as 1A.

The camera’s menu

  • Moves To… gives the camera a move: a dolly, a push, a track. Its stops show as Camera Positions.
  • Add a Pan (R) turns the camera on the spot.
  • Show Lenses on the Plan draws what each lens takes in. See below.
  • Follow… keeps the camera pointed at a character, or at a spot you tap.
  • Track With… makes the camera travel with a character. See below.
  • Describe the Shot… and Add Version… fill in the shot list.
  • Add Storyboard Frame… attaches a frame to the shot.
  • Plays in Time sets when the camera move happens.
  • Camera Details… opens a panel with the shot’s fields, its versions, the camera letter, and the setup’s number and color.

Describe the shot

Where: the camera’s menu › Describe the Shot…. The same fields are on the Shot List tab.
  • Shot Size, Shot Type, Shot Angle and Movement are chosen from lists. Description is your own words.
  • Show Descriptions on the Floor Plan prints them beside each camera.
  • Add Version… gives one camera position a second shot, such as a tighter size from the same spot. Remove This Version takes one away.
  • Delete From Shot List removes the shot.

Lenses on the plan

Where: the camera’s menu › Show Lenses on the Plan.
  • Choose the camera’s lens in the menu: 25, 35, 50, 85, 100, 135, or More…
  • The wedge shows how wide that lens sees.
  • Sensor sets the camera’s sensor size, which changes the width of every wedge.
  • Reach to a Subject or a Spot… stretches the wedge to a character and keeps it on them.
  • Hide Lenses on the Plan turns the wedges off.

Track With

For a camera that travels with a walk: leading, beside or trailing.

Where: the camera’s menu › Track With… › tap the character.
  • The camera gets a position for each of the character’s, in the same Time, so they move together.
  • Choose the side, how far off in feet, and whether it rides Leading, Beside or Trailing.
  • Lay dolly track along it puts track under the move in 4 ft sections.
  • Change the walk and the camera follows.

What it will not do: Leading rides ahead and to the side, not straight in front. And once you drag the camera yourself it stops traveling with the character and keeps its positions. Use Track With… again to relink it.

Storyboard frames

  • Add Storyboard Frame… on a camera attaches a picture to that shot.
  • Frames on the toolbar, or B, shows or hides them on the plan.
  • The folder’s Overview uses each scene’s first and last frames in its Cuts view.

Light and Time of Day

Where the sun will be for an exterior, what a night scene looks like, and film lights on the plan.

In this chapter
  1. Sun & Shadows
  2. Night Look
  3. Lighting pieces

Sun & Shadows

For exteriors. It shows where the sun is at a place, date and time, the shadows it casts, and how each camera is lit.

Where: Layers › Sun & Shadows. It is set for one scene at a time.
  1. Choose the Place. Type a city, tap Use Where I Am, or enter Coordinates…
  2. Check the Date. It comes from the scene’s shoot date in Scene Info, or you can set it here.
  3. Turn north with the slider until the N arrow on the plan points the right way.
  4. Drag Time to move the sun.

The panel then tells you:

  • Where the sun is coming from, how high it is, and how long the shadows are.
  • Sunrise, high point, sunset and the golden hours.
  • How each camera is lit at that time: Front-lit, Side-lit, Looking into the sun, High sun or Sun is down. It also gives the hours when that camera would be front-lit.

Smaller shrinks the panel to a bar with the time on it. Hide Sun turns it off.

What it will not do: Shadows are drawn from typical heights, such as 9 ft for a wall, and only for what is on the plan. A building or a tree you have not drawn casts no shadow. The place must be on the city list, your own position, or coordinates.

Night Look

For night scenes. The plan goes dark and stays lit only where its lights throw.

Where: Layers › Night Look. It is set for one scene at a time.

What lights the dark:

  • Film lights from Add Lighting, along their beams.
  • A pool around a Practical Lamp, Floor Lamp, Street Lamp, Candle, Campfire, Fire or Fireplace.
  • A Flashlight.
  • Vehicles, once you switch on Headlights On in the vehicle’s menu. The switch appears while Night Look is on.

Darkness sets how dark the rest of the plan is.

  • Turning on Night Look turns Sun & Shadows off, and the other way around.
  • The PDF export has a Night Look option, for scenes that have it turned on.

Lighting pieces

Where: Add › Add Lighting…, or press L.
  • The pieces are Fresnel, LED Panel, Softbox, Kino Flo, Practical Lamp and Sun / Window Light.
  • The film lights draw a beam on the plan. Turn the light to aim it.
  • The light’s menu › Details… has a Beam slider for how far it reaches. All the way down hides the beam.
  • Lighting has its own row in Layers, so you can fade it or lock it.

The Shot List tab

Where: the Shot List tab. V switches between the plan and the list. On the Floor Plan, the Shot List panel at the right shows the same shots.
  • Each camera, and each version along its move, is a row.
  • Tap a cell to change it. Size, type, angle and movement come from lists. The description is typed.
  • + Add frame attaches a storyboard frame to the shot.
  • Tap a shot’s number to see that camera on the plan.
  • Drag ⠿ to change the order of the rows.

Columns

Where: Shot list ▾ › Columns….
  • The list starts with eight: Storyboard, Shot #, Version, Shot size, Shot type, Shot angle, Movement and Description.
  • You can turn on Nickname, Lens, Gear, Cast, Props, Est. time, Priority, Flag, VFX, SFX, Status and Notes.
  • New custom column… adds one of your own.
  • Drag ⠿ to change the order, or drag a heading sideways in the list itself.
  • The choice covers every scene in the folder, on all your devices. The PDF shows the same columns in the same order.

Start a scout

  2. Type the Location name.
  3. Tap Start the scout.

The tabs and the shot list step aside, and the scout’s panel takes the right side. ＋ New starts another scout. Other scouts in the folder are listed at the bottom of the panel.

Where: Link scenes… at the bottom of the scout’s panel.

The blocking as a video

Where: Menu › Export › Save the Blocking as a Video…, or Save as Video… in a Time’s menu.
  • It records the page you are on, playing in real time. Keep the app open until it is done.
  • The video is 1280 × 720, with the scene’s name and the current Time across the top.
  • It is saved as MP4 where the browser can make one, and WebM where it cannot.

What it will not do: It needs something that moves. Give a character or a camera a second position and set its Time first.

Who can sync

Where: tap the status at the top right › Approved people…
  • With the lock on, only you and the people on the list can sync.
  • Add each person’s exact email. They tap Create account in the app with that email.
  • Anyone else can still use the app. Their work stays on their own device.
  • Removing someone stops their syncing. Their scenes stay on their devices.

What it will not do: Approving someone does not show them your scenes. Each account syncs its own work to its own devices. To hand someone your plans, send them the PDFs.

What syncs

Once you sign in, these travel to every device on your account:

  • Scenes, with their sets, blocking, cameras and shots.
  • The writing on the Breakdown and Strategy tabs.
  • Images: storyboard frames, plans and scout photos.
  • Folders, locations and scouts.
  • My Props, and your choice of shot list columns.

These stay on the device where you made them:

  • Earlier Versions, and the copies made with Save a Version Now.
  • Anything too large to sync, which the Ready for Set check names. A very long sketch is the usual cause.

When two devices have both been working, new material from both is kept. Only when the same scene was changed on both does the newest change win.
